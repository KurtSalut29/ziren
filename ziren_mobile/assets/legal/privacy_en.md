# Data Privacy Notice

**Version 1.1 — effective 8 October 2026**

Ziren is an emergency incident reporting and dispatch system for the Province
of Biliran. This notice explains what personal information we collect, why we
need it, and what you can do about it. It is written to meet the Data Privacy
Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).

Please read it before you create an account.

## 1. Who is responsible for your data

The Bureau of Fire Protection, the Philippine National Police, and the
Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Offices operating in Biliran
act jointly as the personal information controllers for this system.

## 2. What we collect

**When you create an account**

- Your name, date of birth, and sex
- Your mobile number and email address
- Your address: municipality, barangay, purok or sitio, and street
- A photograph of a valid ID, and the ID type and number
- A photograph of your face, taken during the identity check

**If you tell us**

- Whether you are a person with disability, your PWD ID number, and the type
  of disability
- How you prefer to be contacted
- Your emergency contact's name and number

**When you report an emergency**

- What you write or say in the report, including voice recordings
- Photographs or video you attach
- Your device's location at the time of the report
- The date and time of the report

**Automatically**

- Your device's language setting
- Basic technical information needed to deliver notifications to your phone

## 3. Why we need it

**To send help to the right place.** Your location and address decide which
station is dispatched. This is the core purpose of the system.

**To let responders reach you.** A crew on the way to an incident may need to
call or message you to find you.

**So responders arrive prepared.** If you have told us you are deaf or have a
speech disability, that reaches the crew before they arrive, so they do not
attempt voice contact and waste minutes at your door.

**To confirm you are a real person in Biliran.** False emergency reports send
crews away from real emergencies. Your ID and face photograph let an
administrator confirm your account is genuine.

**To improve the service.** We review reports to make dispatch faster. Any
analysis of this kind uses information with names and contact details removed.

## 4. Reporting an emergency never requires verification

You can submit an emergency report the moment your account exists, whether or
not your ID has been reviewed and whether or not it was ever submitted.
Verification affects only what a dispatcher sees about how confident we are in
your identity. It is never a permission to ask for help.

## 5. Who can see your information

- **Dispatchers and administrators** at the responding agency see your report,
  your contact details, and your accessibility information.
- **The responding crew** sees what they need to find you and arrive prepared.
- **Verifying administrators** see your ID photograph and your face photograph.
  Responders cannot see these — they play no part in identity checks.
- **Nobody else.** We do not sell your information, share it for advertising,
  or give it to anyone outside the emergency services, except where a law or a
  lawful court order requires it.

## 6. How long we keep it

- **Your ID photograph** is deleted once an administrator has finished
  checking it. If nobody reviews your account within 90 days, it is deleted
  anyway. Only the ID type and number remain, as a record of what was checked.
- **Your face photograph** is deleted too if your ID is not approved. If it is
  approved, it is kept as the picture on your Ziren ID - the resident card
  administrators open to recognise you - and is seen only by administrators,
  never by responders or the public. It is deleted with your account.
- **Incident reports** are kept for five years, as operational records of the
  emergency service.
- **Your account** is kept until you ask us to delete it.

## 7. How it is protected

Your information is stored on encrypted servers. Access is restricted by role,
so a responder cannot open records they have no operational reason to see. ID
and face photographs are held in private storage that is never served on a
public address, and are opened only through short-lived links issued to an
administrator at the moment of review.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal information,
we will notify you and the National Privacy Commission as the law requires.

## 8. Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act you have the right to:

- **Be informed** that your data is being processed
- **Access** a copy of the information we hold about you
- **Correct** anything that is inaccurate
- **Object** to processing, or withdraw consent
- **Erasure or blocking** of your data where the law allows it
- **Damages** for harm caused by false, unlawfully obtained, or unauthorised
  use of your information
- **Data portability** — a copy of your data in a usable electronic format
- **Complain** to the National Privacy Commission

You can withdraw your ID photograph from the app at any time without deleting
your account.

Withdrawing consent altogether means we can no longer operate an account for
you, because the system cannot dispatch help to someone whose location and
contact details it may not process. Your past incident reports remain as
operational records of the emergency service.

## 9. Children

An account requires you to be at least 18. A parent or guardian may register
on behalf of a minor, and the barangay may register on behalf of a resident
who cannot complete the form themselves. Where that happens, the system
records who registered the account.

## 10. Contacting us

For any request about your data, or to reach the Data Protection Officer,
contact your municipal MDRRMO office.
